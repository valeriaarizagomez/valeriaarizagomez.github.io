I am a PhD candidate in Science Communication at the Technische Universität München. 
My current research examines how mass media constructs minoritized and dominant groups in the US, the UK, Germany and Poland.
I use computational text analysis to identify broad patterns of representation and critical discourse approaches to analyze the power dynamics present in everyday news.

My experience working alongside government organizations and NGOs lead me to my interest in political culture and how it is constructed. As part of the Emmy Noether Research Group the studies I lead have one starting point: challenging the idea that "inclusion went too far". 




Many of the features of dynamic content management systems (like Wordpress) can be achieved in this fashion, using a fraction of the computational resources and with far less vulnerability to hacking and DDoSing. You can also modify the theme to your heart's content without touching the content of your site. If you get to a point where you've broken something in Jekyll/HTML/CSS beyond repair, your Markdown files describing your talks, publications, etc. are safe. You can rollback the changes or even delete the repository and start over - just be sure to save the Markdown files! You can also write scripts that process the structured data on the site, such as [this one](https://github.com/academicpages/academicpages.github.io/blob/master/talkmap.ipynb) that analyzes metadata in pages about talks to display [a map of every location you've given a talk](https://academicpages.github.io/talkmap.html).

For those users that need more advanced functionality, the template also supports the following popular tools:
- [MathJax](https://www.mathjax.org/) for mathematical equations
- [Mermaid](https://mermaid.js.org/) for diagraming
- [Plotly](https://plotly.com/javascript/) for plotting
