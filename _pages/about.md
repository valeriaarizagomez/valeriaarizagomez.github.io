I am a PhD candidate in Science Communication at the Technical University of Munich (TUM) in the Professorship for Science and Crisis Communication. My supervisors are Prof. Stefanie Walter, PhD. and Prof. Dr. Manon Westphal.
My research examines how mass media constructs and represents minoritized and dominant groups across the US, the UK, Germany, and Poland. I combine computational text analysis with critical discourse approaches to identify patterns of representation, trace changes in media narratives, and examine the power dynamics embedded in everyday news.
My interest in political culture and its construction stems partly from my experience working with government organizations and NGOs. As a member of the Emmy Noether Research Group, my research is guided by a common question: Who gets to define the boundaries of inclusion and belonging? My work challenges the assumption that inclusion has (ever) gone too far in the public sphere, by examining how minoritized groups are represented, constructed, and positioned in public discourse.
I am particularly interested in the intersections of media, politics, social inequality, and in using text as data approaches to better understand how societies make sense of difference. Through computational methods I build the broader context for in depth critical discourse approaches. These combination of methods is crucial to the questions I want to answer. 


Research interests: Media representation · Political communication · Minoritized groups · Symbolic boundaries · Computational social science 

Methods: Computational text analysis · Topic modelling · Stance analysis · Critical Discourse Approaches
